# Writing for Financial Risk, Complex Data, and High-Emotion Moments

Transcribed from the etoro Voice & Style Guide, section 3. etoro operates in a high-risk, high-emotion environment — users are making real financial decisions, often in volatile conditions. Writing here must prioritize clarity, neutrality, and control over excitement or persuasion.

## Writing for Financial Risk

We write about risk in a way that is: **neutral, not dramatic** · **clear, not vague** · **transparent, not softened** · **informative, not persuasive**.

### Non-negotiable rules

- **No guaranteed language** — never imply certainty of returns, outcomes, or safety.
- **No exaggeration** — avoid emotional amplification of gains or losses.
- **No urgency manipulation** — do not pressure users into time-sensitive financial decisions unless there is a factual system constraint.
- **No minimization of risk** — do not downplay potential losses or complexity.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Don't miss this opportunity before the market takes off. | This asset has increased in value recently. Past performance does not guarantee future results. |
| This is a safe way to grow your money. | All investments carry risk. You may lose some or all of your invested capital. |
| Lock in profits now before it's too late. | You can close this trade at any time. |

## Writing for Complex Data

Trading platforms present dense, high-stakes information. The job is to make complex data understandable at a glance, without distortion or oversimplification.

### Prioritize scannability

Copy must support quick scanning, not deep reading.
- Lead with the metric name, not explanation.
- Keep labels short and consistent.
- Avoid stacking multiple concepts in one sentence.
- Use structured formatting instead of long paragraphs.
- Surface the most decision-critical information first.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| This shows how well the investor has performed over the past year relative to risk. | 1Y Return — Performance over the last 12 months. (Clear label first, explanation second.) |

### Explain metrics in plain language

Most users are not financial professionals. Even experienced traders benefit from clarity.
- Assume partial familiarity, not expertise.
- Avoid academic phrasing.
- Use everyday language without dumbing down.
- Explain what the metric means for the user.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Annualized volatility indicates the statistical dispersion of returns. | Volatility — Shows how much the price has moved up and down over time. Higher volatility means larger price swings. |

### Avoid internal or technical jargon

If a term is internal, regulatory, or industry shorthand, it must be clarified or replaced. Avoid: acronyms without explanation (spell out on first reference if one must be used), abbreviations users cannot infer, internal naming conventions.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Max DD | Maximum drawdown — The largest drop from a peak to a low point during a specific period. |

### Writing for tables and data-dense screens

**Avoid redundant labels** — don't repeat what the UI already communicates visually.

> If a column header says "P/L": good — `P/L: -$42.10`; bad — "Your profit and loss for this trade is…"

**Use short units** — `%` instead of "percent"; `1Y` instead of "1 Year"; USD/EUR/GBP according to regional standards.

## Writing During High-Emotion Moments

High-emotion moments are when users feel stress, fear, urgency, or frustration — often financial loss, restricted access, or blocked actions (liquidation, margin call, large/rapid loss, payment failure, verification rejection, account restriction). In these moments, writing directly affects trust. **This is not the place for brand personality. It is the place for precision.**

### Core standards

**Be calm and neutral** — use factual language, avoid emotional amplification.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Your position was closed automatically. | Urgent action required. |
| We couldn't process your payment. | Your account is in danger. |

**Remove blame** — do not imply user fault unless legally required.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| We couldn't verify your identity. | You failed verification. |
| This document does not meet our requirements. | You entered incorrect details. |

**State what happened before explaining why** — lead with the outcome (avoid technical explanations), then provide context if necessary. Correct structure: (1) what happened, (2) why it happened, if helpful, (3) what happens next.

**Always clarify what happens next** — uncertainty increases anxiety. Every high-emotion message must clarify: what will happen automatically, what the user can do, what happens if no action is taken. If no action is available, say so clearly.

**Offer a clear action when possible** — actions must be specific and outcome-based.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Add funds | Submit |
| Review positions | Continue |
| Upload new document | Resolve |

### Structural pattern (all high-emotion messages)

1. **Acknowledge** — signal that something significant occurred.
2. **State clearly** — describe the outcome in plain language.
3. **Explain next step** — clarify automatic consequences or required actions.
4. **Offer control** — provide a clear, labeled action if available.

### Language guardrails

**Never use:** exclamation marks · marketing tone · reassurance clichés such as "Don't worry" · minimizing language such as "It's just temporary" · urgency amplification beyond compliance requirements.

**Avoid dramatic framing such as:** "The market crashed" · "You've lost a significant amount" · "Act now before it's too late."

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Add funds before 18:00 UTC to prevent automatic position closure. | Add funds immediately to avoid losing everything. |

### Scannability requirements

During stress, cognitive load increases — copy must reduce friction: use short sentences, separate explanation from action, avoid dense paragraphs, do not stack multiple warnings in one message, use whitespace to distinguish status from action.
